Project guides

Clean the surface you have, not the one in a rental-store brochure.

Mid-Missouri homes deal with clay, pollen, algae, shade, and weathered materials. Use these checks before you point a washer at siding, concrete, wood, or brick.

Concrete driveways and sidewalks

Sweep first, pre-treat oil or rust, then use a surface cleaner or 25 degree tip in overlapping passes. Rinse toward drainage and keep the pattern consistent.

Talk driveway cleaning with G&S

Siding and house washing

Use detergent and low pressure. Rinse downward, keep water out of laps, vents, outlets, and window seals, and stop if siding looks chalky.

Know when siding needs G&S

Wood and composite decks

Use deck-safe cleaner, a wide fan, and low pressure with the grain. High pressure can scar boards, lift fibers, and leave permanent wand marks.

Review deck cleaning safety

Brick, stone, and pavers

Check mortar, joints, and loose areas before washing. Older masonry and pavers often need a gentler method than direct pressure.

Work order

Move from fragile areas to tougher flatwork.

Dial in your cleaner, distance, and spray pattern on lower-risk surfaces before tackling heavy concrete staining or larger areas.

  1. Protect plants, outlets, lighting, and door gaps.
  2. Pre-treat organic growth, oil, or rust before rinsing.
  3. Let detergent dwell, but do not let it dry.
  4. Rinse at low pressure first; increase only after a clean test area.
  5. Final rinse windows, trim, concrete edges, and plants.
