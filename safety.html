Safety

Pressure washing safety for Mid-Missouri homes.

Inspect the equipment, establish a clear work area, protect vulnerable fixtures, and keep the spray under control. Stop immediately when paint lifts, wood fibers rise, mortar sheds, siding chalks, or water enters an opening.

Protect yourself

Wear eye protection, gloves, long pants, and closed-toe shoes. Keep children and pets outside the work area. Pressure-washer wounds can force water, bacteria, and debris beneath the skin and require prompt medical attention.

Protect the house

Keep spray away from outlets, meters, lights, cameras, HVAC components, vents, window seals, door thresholds, and siding laps. A temporary cover does not make electrical equipment safe to spray.

Protect the surface

Inspect the surface and test an inconspicuous area. Stop if paint lifts, wood fibers rise, mortar sheds, joint sand moves, or siding releases oxidation.

High-risk conditions

Pressure-washing practices to avoid

Do not aim the wand at people, pets, or your own feet.

Pressure can break skin and drive debris into tissue.

Do not use the red tip as a cleaning shortcut.

A pinpoint stream can cut lines into wood, concrete, paint, and siding.

Do not wash from a ladder.

Kickback, hose movement, and wet footing significantly increase fall risk.

Do not spray electrical equipment.

Stay away from outlets, lights, meters, panels, cameras, and HVAC components.

Do not pressure wash roofs.

Roof stains usually call for soft washing, proper access, and fall protection.

Do not keep increasing pressure on a stubborn stain.

Identify the contamination and use a compatible cleaner or request professional guidance.

Request professional help for height, electrical exposure, or fragile materials.
