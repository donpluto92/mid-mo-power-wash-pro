Safety

A pressure washer can cut, lift, and force water where it does not belong.

Treat every spray pattern like it can injure skin, scar materials, or open a path for water. Most damage happens when someone keeps going after the surface is already warning them.

Protect yourself

Wear eye protection, gloves, long pants, and closed-toe shoes. Keep kids and pets away and never point the wand at anyone.

Protect the house

Cover outlets, avoid fixtures and meters, and keep spray out of vents, window seals, door thresholds, and siding laps.

Protect the surface

Test first. If paint lifts, wood fuzzes, mortar sheds, or siding chalks, stop and switch methods.

Never do this

High-risk pressure washing mistakes

Do not aim the wand at people, pets, or your own feet.

Pressure can break skin and drive debris into tissue.

Do not use the red tip as a cleaning shortcut.

A pinpoint stream can cut lines into wood, concrete, paint, and siding.

Do not wash from a ladder.

Kickback, hose pull, and wet footing are a bad mix.

Do not spray electrical equipment.

Stay away from outlets, lights, meters, panels, cameras, and HVAC components.

Do not pressure wash roofs.

Roof stains usually call for soft washing, proper access, and fall protection.

Do not chase stains with more force.

Use the right cleaner or get advice before turning a stain into surface damage.

If the risk is above ground level or behind a wall, pause.
