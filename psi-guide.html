PSI Guide

Match pressure to the material, not the mess.

More pressure is rarely the best answer. Use detergent, dwell time, nozzle angle, and steady technique before turning up the PSI.

Surface Starter PSI Nozzle Notes
Concrete driveway2,500-3,50025 degree or surface cleanerPre-treat oil and rust. Keep the wand moving to avoid stripes.
Pavers and patios1,500-2,50025 or 40 degreeAvoid blasting joint sand. Rinse from high to low.
Vinyl siding500-1,50040 degree or soft washDo not spray upward into laps, vents, or window seals.
Painted wood500-1,20040 degreeTest first. Too much pressure can strip paint and raise grain.
Composite deck800-1,50040 degreeUse manufacturer guidance when available. Skip turbo nozzles.
Natural wood deck500-1,20040 degreeUse wood cleaner and low pressure. Sanding may be needed after cleaning.
Brick in good condition500-1,50025 or 40 degreeAvoid old mortar joints. Use soft washing for delicate brick.
Outdoor furniture500-1,20040 degreeUse low pressure, especially on fabric, wicker, and aged wood.

These are homeowner starting ranges, not guarantees. Always read your machine manual, test a hidden spot, and use the lowest effective pressure.

Soft wash zone

Some jobs need chemistry, not force.

Algae, mildew, oxidation, and organic staining often clean better with the right detergent and a gentle rinse. That is especially true for siding, trim, gutters, and painted surfaces.

Call G&S before using high pressure on:

  • Roofs or second-story siding
  • Oxidized vinyl or aluminum
  • Stucco, EIFS, or old masonry
  • Lead paint risk or peeling paint
