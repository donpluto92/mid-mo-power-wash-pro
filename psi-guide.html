PSI guide

Choose the right pressure for Mid-Missouri exterior surfaces.

Safe working pressure depends on the material, its condition, the washer’s flow rate, nozzle size, spray angle, distance, and cleaning solution. Follow manufacturer guidance and test a hidden area before cleaning the full surface.

SurfaceGeneral reference rangeTip or methodField notes
Concrete driveway2,500-3,500Surface cleaner or 25 degree tipPre-treat oil, rust, and tire marks. Keep passes even to avoid striping.
Sidewalks and patios2,000-3,000Surface cleaner or 25 degree tipRinse toward drainage. Watch edges, joints, and nearby doors.
Pavers1,500-2,50025 or 40 degreeAvoid blowing out joint sand. Rinse at a shallow angle.

Natural wood deck500-1,00040 degreeUse wood cleaner, low pressure, and steady passes with the grain.
Brick in good shape500-1,50025 or 40 degreeCheck mortar first. Older brick may need soft washing, not blasting.
Outdoor furniture500-1,00040 degreeUse low pressure on fabric, wicker, paint, and aged wood.

Important: These figures are general reference ranges. Pressure at the surface changes with nozzle size, flow rate, working distance, equipment, and surface condition. Manufacturer instructions take priority. Test a hidden area and stop if the material changes.

Siding and painted surfaces

Begin with inspection and detergent.

Inspect for oxidation, loose panels, damaged seals, peeling paint, and exposed fixtures. Apply a surface-appropriate detergent at low pressure and keep rinse water away from laps, vents, outlets, lights, and window seals.

Request an assessment for:

  • Roofs or second-story siding
  • Oxidized vinyl or aluminum
  • Stucco, EIFS, or old masonry
  • Lead paint risk or peeling paint
