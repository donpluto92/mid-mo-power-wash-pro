PSI guide

Use pressure like a setting, not a solution.

The right PSI depends on the surface, soil, nozzle, distance, and cleaner. Start low, test first, and let the cleaner do some of the work.

Surface Starting pressure Tip or method Field notes
Concrete driveway 2,500-3,500 Surface cleaner or 25 degree tip Pre-treat oil, rust, and tire marks. Keep passes even to avoid striping.
Sidewalks and patios 2,000-3,000 Surface cleaner or 25 degree tip Rinse toward drainage. Watch edges, joints, and nearby doors.
Pavers 1,500-2,500 25 or 40 degree Avoid blowing out joint sand. Rinse at a shallow angle.
Vinyl siding 500-1,200 Soft wash or 40 degree rinse Apply cleaner at low pressure and rinse downward. Do not drive water behind laps.
Painted wood 500-1,000 40 degree Test carefully. Too much force strips paint and raises grain.
Composite deck 800-1,500 40 degree Follow manufacturer guidance. Skip turbo tips and narrow streams.
Natural wood deck 500-1,000 40 degree Use wood cleaner, low pressure, and steady passes with the grain.
Brick in good shape 500-1,500 25 or 40 degree Check mortar first. Older brick may need soft washing, not blasting.
Outdoor furniture 500-1,000 40 degree Use low pressure on fabric, wicker, paint, and aged wood.

These are starting ranges for homeowners, not guarantees. Conditions vary by machine, nozzle, distance, and surface age. Test a hidden spot and stop if the material changes.

Pressure is not always the answer

House washing usually starts with chemistry.

Algae, mildew, pollen, and oxidation often clean better with the right detergent and a gentle rinse. Siding cleaning, trim, gutters, and painted surfaces can be damaged when force replaces process.

Call G&S before high pressure on:

  • Roofs or second-story siding
  • Oxidized vinyl or aluminum
  • Stucco, EIFS, or old masonry
  • Lead paint risk or peeling paint
